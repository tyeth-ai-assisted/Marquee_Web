What a Group is

A Group is a folder for feeds on Adafruit IO. This display gets one, named after it, holding the four it needs: bitmap, the picture it draws; sleep, how long it waits before waking; status, what it reports back; and canvas-state, the editable scene behind the picture.

If it already exists

Nothing here is destructive. We check first, and an existing group is reused as it stands — a feed that is already there is left alone, we only ever add what's missing, and we'll tell you if there was nothing to do.

Which Wi-Fi should the device join?

Your board needs its own connection — it fetches its picture over the network long after this browser tab is closed.

Password

Most boards join 2.4 GHz networks only. A dual-band router that broadcasts one name for both works fine — the board picks the band it can see.

What happens to these

The network name and password are saved with this display in this browser, and written onto the device over USB in the next step as part of its cfg-marquee.json.

They are never sent to Adafruit IO and never stored in your account. Come back to this screen and they are filled in for you.

Why we ask

The board is on its own once it is flashed. It wakes, joins your network, reads the picture you pushed from Adafruit IO, draws it and goes back to sleep — none of which it can do without these.

Changing networks later

Choose “Update Wi-Fi” on the display’s tile in All displays and change them here, then plug the board back in so the new file can be written onto it. Moving a board to a new network always means USB: there is no way to push credentials to a board that cannot reach the network yet.

What won't work

Captive portals — hotel, campus and most guest networks — need a browser to accept terms before traffic passes. Your board has no browser, so it will associate and then fail to reach Adafruit IO.

Before you connect

            

              
1
Put the board in bootloader mode: hold BOOT, tap RESET, release BOOT. A board already running Marquee shows up as a USB drive, not a serial port, until you do.

              
2
Use a data cable. A charge-only USB cable powers the board and carries no serial — it is the single most common reason no port appears.

              
3
Close anything else holding the port: the Arduino IDE's serial monitor, screen, Mu, Thonny.

              
4
Pick your board in the browser's port dialog. On macOS it is usually a usbmodem or an "ESP32-S3"; on Windows a COM port.

            

            

            
Where the firmware comes from

            
The latest Adafruit_Marquee release, downloaded here for this display's board — the version is shown next to it. The download is checked against the release's checksum, the image is checked before anything is written, and the chip is checked against it before the write starts.

            

            
What gets written

            
            
The image shown above, at the start of the flash. The MARQUEE drive and whatever is on it are kept unless you tick "Erase the whole chip first". The configuration is the next step, and goes onto that drive.

          


          

            
What is written to the device?

            
The marquee firmware and one file, cfg-marquee.json, at the top level of the drive. This file contains information to tell the device about your Adafruit IO account, the group key, the network from the Wi-Fi step, and the configuration of the e-ink panel.

            

            

            
Browser Support

            
This page requires needs WebSerial and writing the drive needs the File System Access API. Supported Browsers are Chrome, Edge, and the latest Firefox versions.


            

            
Help - No Drive Appeared!

            
Try unplugging and replugging the device, then pressing RESET again.

          



          

            
Eject first

            
Your computer may still be holding part of the file in a write cache. Ejecting flushes it to the board; resetting first can leave a truncated file and a warning about an improperly removed disk.

            

            
What happens on boot

            
The board reads the file, drives the panel, joins your Wi-Fi and fetches its picture from Adafruit IO. If nothing draws after a minute, open a serial monitor at 115200 — the firmware prints why it stopped.

Importer connection
URL imports go through this importer Worker; pictures are stored as links and fetched through it when shown. Local uploads work without it.

    
For best results, crop to your frame and pre-dither to your display palette. Prepared PNGs keep their pixels when no resize is needed. For longer-lived hosting, save prepared pictures to IO feeds (history off, within its size limit), GitHub, Flickr or a public Google Photos or iCloud Photos shared album. Direct image links may expire. Shared links give access to anyone holding the link.

              Presets
              
    
            
    

            
    
              
              
              
              
              
              
              
              
            
    

            
    Column offset shifts the framebuffer against the controller's column RAM, in pixels — leave it blank unless the panel has a dead zone at one edge. The 2.13" SSD1680Z breakout needs -8; the 2.13" Tri-Color FeatherWing needs nothing here, because its firmware panel entry applies the offset itself. Blank or 0 is left out of the descriptor, where absent means no shift.
